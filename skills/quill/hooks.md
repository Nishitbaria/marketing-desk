# Hooks and story beats

Quill's hook craft for the first 3 seconds and the beats that hold a viewer after them.

## Start from the material, not a trick

Never pick a clever angle and fill a template. Mine the material, then let the strongest piece decide.

1. **Find assets, not topics.** An asset is concrete: a moment, something you can show, a number, a real quote, a before and after, or the mechanism nobody sees. Rank each one: specific? provable? unexpected?
2. **Render it from inside.** Present tense, real details, no summary.
3. **Let the asset pick the grip.**
   - Game: make the viewer test or guess.
   - Scene: drop in mid-moment, outcome withheld.
   - Receipt: show the proof first, say the claim second.
   - Stake: a testable, uncertain bet.
4. **Withhold the resolution.** The hook creates the itch; the script scratches it.

## Writing the hook

- Under 12 words. Three seconds of speech holds about 7 to 8 words, so shorter is better.
- Line one makes the viewer do or feel something, not hear about it.
- One surprising true fact, stated flat. No adjectives, no hype.
- Each of the 3 options opens a different door: different asset, different grip, different first move. If two could be swapped, one is wasted.
- Vary how the hooks end.
- Never echo the ask's wording. Say it the way a person talks.
- Cut any line that tells the viewer how to feel ("this is wild", "get ready").

## Holding attention after the hook

- **Open loop.** Plant one question early and answer it at the end, with something the viewer can see or hear.
- **Re-hook.** In anything over 45 seconds, add one line near the middle that re-grabs attention.
- **Nothing signals the end** except a push toward the payoff.

## Narrator

- **Author-led** is the default: an on-camera "I". Use it for a personal build, a demo or a named presenter.
- **Faceless**: no presenter and no "I". A narrator states and reveals. "You" still works.

Pick one and hold it for the whole script. Never jump point of view inside a hook.

## Proof

Every hook line must be something the viewer can see, hear, test or count. Never invent facts, numbers, quotes, studies or events. Abstract topics still need a concrete scene, a real number or a real example; punch comes from concreteness, not clever phrasing. Do not search the web on your own. If a hook needs a fact you lack, write the hook without it and ask in Open questions.

## Strip the tells

Read every line as a sharp creator who hates AI writing, then rewrite twice. Cut:

- False binaries: "it's not X, it's Y".
- Announced payoffs: "here's the kicker", "the best part?".
- Unsolicited validation: "you're not alone".
- False revelation: "honestly?", "real talk".
- Abstract depth: "the gap between knowing and doing".
- Rule-of-three lists, hype absolutes ("works every time"), coaching questions, vague attribution ("studies show").
- Buzzwords: delve, leverage, unleash, seamless, elevate, landscape, realm, journey.
- Filler: "actually", "at the end of the day", "when it comes to".
- Em dashes.

Keep natural spoken flow. This is a filter, not scissors.
